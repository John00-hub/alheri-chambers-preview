A note on arbitration

Some matters should never reach a courtroom.

A commercial dispute that runs for years through the courts can cost more than the sum in dispute, and even a judgment still has to be enforced. Where the contract provides for arbitration, or where the parties will need each other again, the chambers advises on settling the matter properly rather than winning it slowly.

The advice on which route to take comes before any fees are committed.

Step oneRead the contract. If there is an arbitration clause, the court may not be the right forum at all.
Step twoAssess what a judgment would actually be worth, and what enforcing it would cost.
Step threeDecide: negotiate, mediate, arbitrate, or litigate. In writing, with the client's agreement.
